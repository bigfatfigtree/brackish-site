A More Colorful Coast

4 MIN READHAMPTON ROADS

Drive through the 757 with your eyes open and you'll notice it: the walls are talking. Warehouses, underpasses, the sides of buildings that used to be blank — local artists have been quietly turning Hampton Roads into an open-air gallery, one wall at a time.

Why It Matters

Murals do something no marketing budget can buy: they give a neighborhood a face. A blank wall says "nothing happens here." A forty-foot portrait says the opposite. Cities that get this — and fund it — end up with districts people actually visit on purpose.

The Movement, in Three Walls

The portrait walls. Towering faces watching over downtown blocks — equal parts art and landmark. You navigate by them now: "turn left at the big mural."

The district pieces. Arts districts where every other building is a canvas, and the whole street feels like somewhere.

The underpass galleries. Infrastructure nobody looks at, transformed by somebody who decided to look at it harder than anyone else.

"A city that paints its walls is a city that's planning to stick around."
