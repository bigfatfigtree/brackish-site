The Best Waterfront Spots (That Locals Actually Go To)

5 MIN READHAMPTON ROADS

Every coastal city has two waterfronts: the one on the postcards and the one where locals actually eat. This is the second list. No tourist traps survived our vetting — if the menu has more laminated pages than the food has flavor, it's not here.

The Rules

  • You should be able to see the water from your table. Non-negotiable.
  • If it's packed with bachelorette parties on a Tuesday, it's disqualified.
  • Bonus points for dockage — arrive by boat, leave smug.

The Short List

The dockside dive. You know the one — plastic chairs, paper menus, the best crab cakes of your life. Every inlet in the 757 has one. Find yours and guard it.

The golden-hour deck. String lights, a raw bar, and a sunset that does half the work. Get there before 6 or don't get a table.

The hidden gem. Down a road you'd never turn onto, behind a marina you'd never visit. The food is better than it has any right to be. We're not naming it — some secrets are load-bearing.

"A waterfront restaurant with a bad view is just a restaurant with higher rent."

— The Editor
